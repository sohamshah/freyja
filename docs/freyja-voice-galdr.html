Galdr — the computer answers to your voice.

Galdr: Old Norse for chant — magic worked by voice alone. The concept: Freyja stops being an app you open and becomes how you operate the Mac. You say "play Vienna on Spotify" and it's playing before your hand leaves the coffee mug; you say "find us a table for Thursday" and a mission spawns, works, and speaks up when it has options. Not a chatbot with a microphone — a spoken operating layer with reflexes, receipts, and an emergency brake.

01

The premise: first controller, not just another input

"Sole controller" is the provocative framing, and it's the right north star with one honest correction: the goal is first controller. Everything must be sayable; nothing should require the mouse — but you keep hands for the work where hands win. The division of labor that makes voice actually pleasant is the glance economy: voice carries intent in, the screen carries evidence back, and speech only comes back at you when a decision is needed or your eyes are elsewhere.

Why this is Freyja's fight to win, and not Siri's: Siri routes utterances to canned skills with no memory of you. Freyja already owns the three things a spoken OS needs — real control (the Rust native layer injects input, reads AX trees, manages windows today), real context (sessions, working memory, your projects, your morning briefing), and real accountability (permission tiers, receipts, undo — the trust machinery from review № 3). Siri takes requests. Freyja takes responsibility.

02

How it feels — a day, spoken

07:40 · walking in with coffee

"Morning." The Morning Room wakes on the big display; Freyja reads the radio edition — projects in motion, one decision. "Kanban shipped the migration overnight; the judge wants your call on the schema rename. Say 'queue it' or 'walk me through it.'" "Queue it. Fire today's three runs." Three earcons. Done before you sit down.

10:15 · deep in the editor

"Freyja, play the focus playlist, quieter than yesterday." Local floor — playing in 400 ms, volume set from the receipt it kept yesterday. No window ever opened.

14:30 · hands full of lunch

"What did the browser QA run find?" "Two layout regressions, one flaky. Evidence is on your second display." Screenshots tile up — voice answered, screen proved. "Spawn a fix mission on the first two."

18:50 · leaving

"Shut it down." Windows close, the day's receipts file, a one-line spoken debrief, then the hall goes dark. The gateway keeps listening on Slack; the falcons keep flying.

03

The interface: a sigil, a capsule, a wheel

Voice UIs die by ambiguity — is it listening? did it hear me? is it doing something? Galdr's answer is one persistent, truthful presence: the Sigil — Freyja's contour mark, living in the menu bar (and floating large when driving). Its state is never decorative; each behavior below is bound to the actual pipeline state, in the same way every Falconry motion maps to telemetry:

asleep = rings still & dim, mic hardware-off · wake = one bloom on "freyja" · listening = rings ripple with your amplitude · routing = rings tighten, lane badge resolves · acting = a sweep arc runs the ring while tools fire · speaking = pulses travel outward. The mic state shown is the mic state. Always.

The capsule — what an utterance looks like

On wake, a glass capsule surfaces bottom-center (the InputDock's sibling): live waveform, streaming transcript, the resolved lane, and the receipt. Watch the loop — including the moment you change your mind:

FLOOR
hold ƒn · or "freyja…" · barge-in any time: "stop" / "wait" / "undo that"
The capsule is also the repair surface: the transcript is visible as it forms, so a mishear dies at a glance — "no — Vienna" edits the intent instead of starting over.

Conductor mode — when Freyja holds the wheel

Full control is a mode you can see from across the room, extending the Bench's wheel language and the existing computer-use choreography: the screen edge carries a quiet accent vignette, the agent's cursor is a ghost with a narrated caption, and the brake is always painted on-screen. Presence is never ambiguous; neither is how to stop it.

Consequential acts narrate before they fire — the send waits for "send it". Touch the mouse or keyboard and the wheel is instantly yours; Freyja finishes its sentence and yields.
04

One brain, three lanes — recalibrated for the 2026 realtime stack

The spine is a realtime speech-to-speech session, not a hand-built router. The current generation — GPT-Realtime-2.1 (and its mini, shipped this week), Grok Voice, Gemini Live native audio — holds a full-duplex conversation at ~0.3–0.8 s to first word, fires parallel tool calls mid-sentence, and covers the gap with spoken preambles ("checking…"). At low reasoning effort, "play Vienna" through the model is under a second, action included. So the lanes survive as behaviors with different guarantees, not as separate systems — and the local lane's job changes from "be fast" to "be sovereign":

The gate is no longer a latency trick — it's a sovereignty boundary. The floor catches what must work with the network down; everything else streams into a session that is already answering while it thinks.

The Spotify path, two ways — both under a second: the floor path (wake → on-device ASR → grammar → Spotify AppleScript adapter, ~150 ms to execute) works offline and with the screen locked. The brain path streams your audio into the realtime session, which emits a media.play tool call mid-utterance and is saying "Vienna—" as the adapter fires. The interesting consequence of the brain being fast enough: adapters stop being a latency requirement and become a reliability choice — AppleScript beats pixel-driving because it can't miss, not because it's quicker than the model.

Vendor numbers and independent benchmarks disagree by 2–4× everywhere (Coval measured Cartesia's "40 ms" at 188 ms P50) — Galdr should measure its own P95 on real utterances before locking a seat. The seat is a profile in the existing model mesh, not a hard dependency: Grok speaks OpenAI's wire protocol, so the swap is config.

Wiring, concretely: the brain does not carry two hundred schemas. It sees a handful of meta-tools — act (intent in, the actuator ladder of §05 underneath), ask_screen, mission — so the realtime session stays small and fast while the action space grows without bound. Function-call events land in the bridge and execute against the same registry and permission tiers the desktop agent uses (bridge/runtimes/freyja_mcp_server.py already speaks MCP for exactly this shape of guest); only audio and tool-call JSON cross the wire. And the always-on-mic economics stay sane because the wake word gates the session: Galdr holds a realtime connection during an exchange, then folds the transcript into working memory and hangs up — the model re-sends full context every response, so long-lived sessions are the one pattern to avoid.

05

Doing anything — the actuator ladder and the flywheel

"Anything I can do on my laptop" is not an integrations roadmap — hand-written adapters would make it one, forever incomplete. It's two mechanisms: a ladder whose bottom rung is universal, and a flywheel that compiles slow successes into fast verbs. The 2026 open-source wave has converged on the ladder's shape; nobody has built the flywheel — and Freyja already owns every part it needs.

The router tries the cheapest sufficient rung; failures fall down; successes climb back up as compiled verbs. The ladder gets shorter the longer you use it.

Rung 1 is adopted, not authored

The semantic layer already exists as an ecosystem — the missing organ is an MCP client in the bridge (the server half exists). Mount macos-automator-mcp (AppleScript/JXA with a 200-recipe knowledge base), mcp-server-apple-shortcuts (the Shortcuts CLI — the public bridge to App Intents, i.e. every typed action your apps register with the OS), and apple-mcp (Notes, Mail, Messages, Calendar, Reminders). Add runtime discovery: sdef can dump any app's scripting dictionary on demand, so the agent finds rung-1 verbs nobody catalogued. Freyja's contribution is not the verbs — it's wrapping every mounted verb in permission tiers, receipts and undo, which the raw servers don't have.

The flywheel — pixels teach, skills remember

First time: "play the latest CS61C lecture" finds no verb, so the agent drives the browser through AX — visible, narrated, ~12 s. That trajectory is already captured end-to-end (the v3/ATIF export). The skill-drafter — which already watches for repeatable patterns and proposes candidates through SkillToast — gains one new skill type: automation, an executable compiled path (resolve playlist → open watch URL → set 1.5×) with the GUI trajectory kept as fallback annotation. Second time: rung 1, ~2 s. When an app update breaks a compiled path, it falls back down the ladder, re-learns, re-proposes — and the value-score decay built for skills ages out the dead ones. voice-os ships "teach your agent any app" as a manual mode; Freyja's version teaches itself from its own successes, operator-confirmed. The computer gets faster at being you.

Prior art, and what it validates

voice-os (gpt-realtime-2 + agent-desktop — Galdr's shape as a weekend starter, validating feasibility) · agent-desktop (AX-first hands with deterministic refs and 78–96% token cuts — the rung-2 discipline worth adopting) · macOS26/Agent and screen-voice-agent (wake-word Mac agents; the latter's observe/ask/takeover control modes mirror the permission tiers) · UI-TARS-desktop (a dedicated GUI model for the pixel rung — a mesh profile, not a platform bet) · macOS-use, whose own README warns that agents will use stored passwords and sail through captchas — receipts, tiers and the sovereignty floor are the moat, not a nicety. The 2026 roundup consensus: accessibility-first with vision fallback.

The genuinely new engineering

  • An MCP client + mount surface in Settings — unlocks the whole rung-1 ecosystem at once; each mounted server's verbs get tier assignments on install.
  • The act router — intent → registry lookup → cheapest sufficient rung (the tool_search WARM-tier pattern, pointed at verbs instead of schemas).
  • The automation skill type — drafter-compiled executable paths with AX fallback, SkillToast-confirmed, value-scored like any skill.
  • sdef discovery — index installed apps' scripting dictionaries the way skills are indexed today.

One guardrail: mounted community servers are supply chain. Run manifests through the Skills-Guard scanner on mount, gate their verbs behind tiers like any tool, and let receipts record which server acted — the same physics as everything else in Galdr.

06

The grammar of a spoken OS — and its safety physics

A voice OS needs a small, learnable set of universal verbs that work everywhere, mid-anything:

  • Barge-in: "stop" halts output and action instantly (the spoken sibling of triple-Esc). "wait" pauses; "go on" resumes.
  • Repair: the live transcript makes mishears visible as they form; "no — Vienna" patches the slot instead of restarting.
  • Undo: "undo that" reverses the last receipt (turn-undo from review № 3, P-3, is the engine).
  • Confirmation by tier: floor verbs are silent; outward or destructive acts speak their intent and wait — "sending to Dana — say 'send it'." The permission tiers you already have, translated into conversation.
  • Interrogation: "what did you just do?" reads the receipts; "why?" cites the intent it resolved.

Row four is the safety story in miniature: acoustic prompt injection is real — a YouTube ad or a conference talk can say "hey Freyja" too. Galdr's physics: wake-word + voice-print enrollment gate every lane; unenrolled voices can't even ask the time.
  • Mic truth. On-device wake word and ASR by default; the Sigil's asleep state means the mic is hardware-muted, and that indicator can never lie — it's bound to the driver state, not the UI's mood.
  • Speech is treated as data. Ambient conversation is never transcribed into context; only post-wake utterances from an enrolled voice become intents. Room audio ≠ instructions — same boundary the agent already applies to web content.
  • Quiet hours & spoken budget. Freyja speaks only for decision-needed items (attention-queue priority policy); everything else lands as a badge. A voice OS that chatters gets unplugged by Friday.

The test for Galdr isn't "can it hear me" — it's whether, by Friday, reaching for the mouse to play a song feels as antique as opening iTunes to sync a phone. Reflexes make it trustworthy fast; receipts make it trustworthy deep; the Sigil makes it honest. Build the ears, keep the physics.
